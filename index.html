Why Fire Safety Matters So Much Here

Nepal's dense urban housing, aging wiring, LPG kitchens and dry winters create a uniquely high fire risk. Yet fire safety awareness remains low — most homes have no extinguisher, no alarm, and no escape plan.

The good news: basic fire safety in Nepal is affordable and simple. A certified extinguisher, a smoke alarm and a family drill cost less than a smartphone — and they protect everything you own.

  • Electrical short-circuits are the #1 cause of urban fires
  • LPG and kitchen fires spread in seconds without an extinguisher nearby
  • Early detection with alarms doubles survival chances
  • Professional guidance is available across Nepal

Common Fire Hazards in Nepali Homes & Businesses

Recognizing these everyday risks is the first step of real fire safety.

🔌

Faulty Electrical Wiring

Overloaded circuits, loose connections and cheap extension boards spark countless fires each year. Get wiring inspected and never daisy-chain heaters.

🍳

Kitchen & LPG Fires

Unattended cooking and leaking gas pipes are deadly. Keep a fire extinguisher within reach of every kitchen.

🕯️

Candles, Diyo & Heaters

Open flames during load-shedding hours and winter heaters near curtains ignite fast. Never leave them unattended.

🏭

Industrial & Workshop Risks

Flammable liquids, welding sparks and dust need engineered protection — hydrant systems and suppression for high-risk sites.

🔥

Dry Season Wildfires

Spring wildfires threaten hillside settlements. Clear dry brush around property and report smoke immediately.

🏢

Blocked Exits in Buildings

Locked stairwells and cluttered corridors trap people. Every building needs marked, clear escape routes and fire safety signage.

7 Practical Fire Safety Tips for Every Home

Small habits that make fire safety in Nepal a daily practice, not a one-time purchase.

  • Install smoke detectors in kitchens, hallways and stairwells — test monthly
  • Keep an ABC-type fire extinguisher on each floor; learn PASS (Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep)
  • Switch off heaters and chargers before sleeping or leaving home
  • Store LPG cylinders upright, outdoors or ventilated — check pipes for leaks
  • Keep a metal-lidded bucket of sand near diyo/candle spots during festivals
  • Plan two escape routes from every room; practice with family twice a year
  • Save emergency numbers: fire brigade 101, police 100

If Fire Breaks Out — Act in Seconds

Panic kills. These four steps save lives when every second counts.

1

Alert Everyone

Shout "Fire!" and trigger the alarm. Wake the whole house or floor immediately.

2

Try to Contain

If the fire is small and you have an extinguisher, use PASS. Never risk yourself for property.

3

Evacuate Low

Stay low under smoke, feel doors before opening, and use stairs — never elevators.

4

Call 101

Once outside, call the fire brigade. Never go back inside for belongings.

Fire Safety FAQs — Nepal

Common questions about fire safety in Nepal, answered.

What is the most important fire safety device for a home in Nepal?

An ABC-type fire extinguisher plus a smoke alarm. Together they cover detection and first response — the two pillars of fire safety.

How do I improve fire safety in my apartment building?

Push for extinguishers on every floor, clear stairwells, a building-wide alarm, and an annual drill. For larger complexes, a fire hydrant system may be required.

Where can I buy certified fire safety equipment in Nepal?

Nicofire supplies ISO-certified extinguishers, alarms and hydrant systems with delivery across Nepal and expert after-sales support.

How often should fire extinguishers be serviced?

Visual checks monthly, professional servicing annually, and refilling after any use — refilling services keep them rescue-ready.

What number do I call for a fire emergency in Nepal?

Call the fire brigade at 101 and police at 100. Save both in every family member's phone today.
